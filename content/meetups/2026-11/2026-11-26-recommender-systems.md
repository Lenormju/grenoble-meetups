Every time you see a product ad, a recommender system has picked a handful of items out of millions — in milliseconds. At Criteo, we build these systems for thousands of retailers worldwide.

Recommendation is increasingly borrowing from language modeling: products become “tokens”, user histories become “sentences”, and LLMs are entering the stack — as content encoders, as generative recommenders over Semantic IDs, and even as models that reason before recommending.

But what really helps in practice? Drawing on research and production experiments at Criteo, this talk walks through when content improve recommendations (and when it does not), what it takes for generative recommenders to compete with classic ID-based models, and whether “thinking before recommending” pays off.

We will close with a question every practitioner faces: how do you know a new model is better before running an expensive A/B test?

No prior recsys background needed.

Speaker: Artem Betlei is a Senior Researcher at Criteo AI Lab, working on recommender systems and user modeling. He holds a PhD from Université Grenoble Alpes on causal machine learning (uplift modeling) and co-supervises PhD and intern research at Criteo.
